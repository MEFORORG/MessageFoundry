- **An SVG or HTML attachment download refuses an `svg` start tag after leading text.** A body whose
  first byte past leading whitespace was not `<` was served as stored, even under an SVG or HTML
  label and even when it carried `<svg onload=...>` further on. Now, under a label a browser
  renders as SVG or HTML (`image/svg+xml` and any label naming svg, `text/html`,
  `application/xhtml+xml`), the first 64 KiB past that leading noise is scanned for an `svg` start
  tag, plain or inflated from gzip, and the body is refused with a 422 when one is found. Under any
  other label, such as `text/plain`, `application/json`, HL7, PDF or DICOM, a quoted `<svg>` is
  data and is served as before. Every such body was already served as an inert type with
  `nosniff`, so this is defence in depth, and what lies past the head rests on that inert-type
  allow-list, as
  [ADR 0105](../docs/adr/0105-streaming-very-large-hl7-attachments-detach-the-opaque-document-from-the-transformable-skeleton.md)
  records. The download's pre-check reads only a bounded head, and the 422 now says the attachment
  carries SVG rather than that it is one. (`BACKLOG #3257`)
