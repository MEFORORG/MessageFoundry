- **A row stranded in flight is recovered without a restart in at least the common cases, and no
  later row on its lane overtakes it there.** A store fault between a committed claim and its
  handoff leaves the row `inflight`, and the worker's re-pend of it can fail too. A worker, or a
  pooled lane serializer, whose re-pend fails now retries it on a capped backoff while it holds
  the lane, claiming nothing newer until the row is pending again, so per-lane order holds. A
  per-lane worker that dies holding a row has the rows on its own lane re-pended before its
  replacement claims anything, and a replacement for a worker that keeps dying waits a doubling
  backoff, up to 30 s. A row that kills its worker every time is dead-lettered once its attempts
  reach a finite `retry_max_attempts` (or, on an outbound, its retry policy's `max_attempts`);
  with no cap it goes on being retried on that backoff. At least two cases are not covered.
  Clustered, the respawn step stands down, as the unconditional startup reset does, and the row
  waits for the lease reclaim or the promotion reset. And a claim that committed but whose reply
  never reached the worker leaves it no record of the row, which stays in flight until a store
  recovery path re-pends it, at least the next start. A stop during the retry leaves the row in flight for
  the next start, as before. While a row stays in flight, the in-flight watch added in 0.5.0
  still pages it. (`BACKLOG #1611`)
