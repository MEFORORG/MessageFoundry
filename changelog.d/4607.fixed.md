- **A worker killed mid-handoff by an error that is not an `Exception` no longer strands its row.**
  The stage workers catch `Exception` only, so such an error killed the task with its claimed row
  in flight. Before, a per-lane worker's replacement then claimed past that row and delivered its
  successors first, and in the pooled claim mode a lane serializer killed that way was not replaced
  at all, so its lane stopped draining. Both lasted until the next start. Now the replacement
  re-pends the rows the dead task held before its lane claims again, with the same hold-and-retry,
  leadership gate and pause and halt ends as a failed re-pend after a store fault, so per-lane order
  holds with no restart. In the pooled claim mode the serializer is respawned for this, and a
  batching outbound's coalesced rows are re-pended with its head. A row that kills its worker every
  time now holds its lane. In the per-lane claim mode the respawn backs off after a repeat death,
  the death is logged on a backoff (the 1st, 2nd, 4th ...), `lane_stuck` is paged once the deaths
  reach `infra_fault_stop_after` with no pass settling between them, and the row is never
  dead-lettered, as a row whose handoff faults every pass is not. In the pooled claim mode each
  death in the body counts as a zero-progress infrastructure fault, so `infra_fault_policy` STOPs
  the lane or pages `lane_stuck` as for any persistent fault, and a run of replacements that die
  themselves pages `lane_stuck` at the same bound. A replacement sends no re-pend once its node no
  longer leads under the lease the rows were claimed under, since a successor may hold them. A stop
  of the engine, and a teardown, still leave the rows for the next start, now with a log line
  saying how many. (`BACKLOG #4607`)
