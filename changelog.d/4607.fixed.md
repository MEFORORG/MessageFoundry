- **A worker killed mid-handoff by an error that is not an `Exception` no longer strands its row.**
  The stage workers catch `Exception` only, so such an error killed the task with its claimed row
  in flight. Before, a per-lane worker's replacement then claimed past that row and delivered its
  successors first, and in the pooled claim mode a lane serializer killed that way was not replaced
  at all, so its lane stopped draining. Both lasted until the next start. Now the replacement
  re-pends the rows the dead task held before its lane claims again, with the same hold-and-retry,
  leadership gate and pause and halt ends as a failed re-pend after a store fault, so per-lane order
  holds with no restart. The re-pend counts as a retry, so a replacement on a node that is not the
  leader holds its lane and waits, as a held retry does, and sends none once the node leads under
  another lease, since a successor may hold the rows. In the pooled claim mode the serializer is
  respawned for this, a batching outbound's coalesced rows are re-pended with its head, and the
  replacement returns its processing slot while it waits. A row that kills its worker every time
  now holds its lane. In the per-lane claim mode the respawn backs off after a repeat death, the
  death is logged on a backoff (the 1st, 2nd, 4th ...), `lane_stuck` is paged once the deaths reach
  `infra_fault_stop_after` with no pass settling between them, and the row is never dead-lettered,
  as a row whose handoff faults every pass is not. In the pooled claim mode a death on the first row
  of the serializer's batch counts as a zero-progress infrastructure fault, so
  `infra_fault_policy` STOPs the lane or pages `lane_stuck` as for any persistent fault; a death
  past the first row counts as progress. The serializer deaths are also counted, and a run of
  `infra_fault_stop_after` of them with no serializer finishing its batch between pages
  `lane_stuck` once, unless that recovery's own fault reaches the bound and alerts instead. A stop
  of the engine leaves the rows for the next start, and a cancelled task leaves them with no log
  line. Where a stop ends a pooled recovery before its re-pend lands, the dispatcher's drain on a
  demotion no longer reports zero rows in flight. (`BACKLOG #4607`)
