- **The SQL Server cluster heartbeat, config-version refresh and stepdown lease release run under
  the renew clamp.** `[cluster].lease_renew_timeout_seconds` now bounds their wait for a pooled
  connection and their statement, as it already bounded the lease claim, so a slow statement
  among them cannot delay the next renew by up to `[store].command_timeout`. Each can still take
  up to about twice the clamp, as the claim can, and about three times when its rollback runs.
  The engine's start-up config-version seed runs under the clamp too, and `stop()`'s lease
  release and `left` write under its own shutdown bound, applied to the driver as well. Whether anything bounds
  the COMMIT after the statement is not known: by a reading of pyodbc's documentation the
  statement timeout does not, and that is not a measurement. At least the start-up registration,
  an operator's config-version bump and the API's membership and lease reads still run under
  `[store].command_timeout`. A clamped borrow that times out names the clamp, not
  `[store].acquire_timeout`, as the setting to retune. The paused tick's owner read is clamped at
  the driver too, at the smaller of the clamp and half its own budget. A SQL Server store statement
  that committed now keeps its result when only the pool release afterwards fails, logged as a
  warning, so a stepdown whose release landed no longer answers `503 release-unconfirmed` and a
  renew that landed no longer reads as failed.
