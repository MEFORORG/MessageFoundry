- **On SQL Server, the session writes that follow a committed auth change now wait out a busy
  pool.** Rotating a session and revoking a user's sessions borrow a store connection without the
  `[store].acquire_timeout` limit, as they already did on Postgres. Before, a pool shortage at that
  limit could fail them after a password reset, a disable, a role change or a completed second
  factor had committed, which left the account's other sessions live or the old token unrotated.
  The statement timeout still applies. **A rotation the store refuses now fails closed on every
  backend.** The old session is revoked, best effort, an `auth.session_rotation_failed` audit row
  with reason `store_error` records whether that revoke landed, and the caller is told to sign in
  again. Before, the error escaped the ceremony and the old token kept working with the factor it
  had just proved. (`BACKLOG #3023`)
