- **An `Http()` listener with `tls = true` bounds how many slots one address may hold in its TLS
  handshake.** Where the engine upgrades TLS itself (the stdlib event loop), a socket takes a
  `max_connections` slot before its handshake. With `max_connections_per_host` off, as it ships on
  `Http()`, one address that never finished a handshake, at least an mTLS peer with no client
  certificate, could have held all 256 slots for up to 10 s each. The new `max_handshakes_per_host`
  (default 32, `None` or `0` turns it off) caps only the sockets one address holds while still in
  their handshake. Over it, the socket is closed unanswered with an `at_capacity` event whose
  reason reads `max_handshakes_per_host`. A socket leaves the count when its handshake ends, so the
  cap never limits established requests, and `max_connections_per_host` still ships off. It does
  not bound a peer that completes its handshake and then sends nothing. Behind an
  L4 load balancer that collapses source addresses the 32 bound every client's handshakes together;
  see [DEPLOYMENT.md](../docs/DEPLOYMENT.md#per-address-listener-caps-behind-the-vip).
