## 1. Verdict

**Grade: A-** — a strong secure-build posture, honestly bounded. It is capped below a full A only by signal 10 (independent external verification).

The reassessment that produced A- is exactly the work this rubric exists to do. A wrongly-pessimistic gap is as much a defect as a wrongly-optimistic one. MEFOR is genuinely strong wherever a machine holds the line.

The durable, enforced layer (signals 1–8) is Built and verified in the real files. It includes:

- seven blocking CI security jobs that fail red-on-regression from a clean baseline (`pip-audit`, `npm-audit`, `bandit`, `gitleaks` full-history, `semgrep --error`, crypto-inventory, forbidden-content);
- hash-locked dependencies with release-time blocking SBOM, Sigstore, and SLSA provenance;
- a fail-closed forbidden-content scanner shared byte-for-byte across `publish.ps1` / pre-commit / CI;
- a deny-by-default loopback bind guard that `--allow-insecure-bind` cannot relax past the no-auth refusal;
- strongest-per-partner interface auth (mTLS / OAuth2-CC / SMART / WS-Security) with TLS everywhere;
- a SHA-256 hash-chained, user-attributed, append-only audit log with dedicated tamper tests.

That layer earns its "Strong" marks.

The process and attestation layer (signals 9–12) is thinner, and it carries the real defects the rubric is built to catch. No independent third-party ASVS review, penetration test, or DAST has run. That is the one gap capping the grade (signal 10). It is held only by a dated, standing risk acceptance required before off-loopback exposure.

Three items had held the grade at B+. All three were resolved 2026-07-14. First, four ASVS scorecards formerly coexisted with conflicting counts; they were reconciled. `ASVS-L3-STATUS.md`, `ASVS-FAILS-REMEDIATION-PLAN.md`, and the prior `ASVS-L3-ASSESSMENT.md` (214/0/0/131) now carry SUPERSEDED banners pointing to the sole verdict-of-record `ASVS-L3-ASSESSMENT-2026-07-09.md` (179 Pass / 46 Partial / 5 Fail), and the register's 12.1.4 was aligned to its Fail (signal 11). Second, the risk-acceptance register that governs those residuals was a v1.0 draft with all eight sign-off blocks blank; it was signed 2026-07-14 across all 8 themes, so those residuals are now accepted and owned (signal 11). Third, at-rest encryption, first read as "off by default," was shown on close code review to be fail-closed for every PHI posture (gap #4 in §3; full mechanism in §2 signal 6). With all three resolved, the composite is A-. The one gap still capping the grade is independent external verification (signal 10), held open under the dated, signed risk acceptance.

Two verification refinements are folded into the rows below, both in the honest direction. Signal 2's "PW.5 all Pass" is overstated; the cited self-review marks three PW.5 rows Partial. Signal 12's release-gate has three weaknesses: the acceptance it leans on was unsigned, the SLSA attestation is skipped while the repo is private, and the 19 tags are unsigned — so its "signed tag + provenance" bar is codified but unexercised. Neither refinement moves the letter grade. Both are logged so the evidence base stays honest.

## 5. Documented caveat — solo-maintainer self-review + loopback-only, pre-1.0 posture

This scorecard is graded under the same declared deviation that governs [Secure_Development_Standards.md](Secure_Development_Standards.md) §A.6 and the [Secure_AI_Development_Standards.md](Secure_AI_Development_Standards.md) §A.6 companion. MessageFoundry is a single-maintainer, pre-1.0, loopback-only project, so two SSDF expectations cannot be met from inside the project and are met by compensating controls instead: human peer review (PW.7) and independent external verification (signal 10 / SDS §6.3).

- **No independent human peer review.** PW.7 is a documented single-maintainer deviation. The compensating controls are the non-waivable blocking SAST/SCA/secret-scan CI gates (signal 3), which cannot be self-approved away, plus AI-assisted adversarial review as a second lens. This is a real mitigation, not an equivalent. The Stanford CCS'23 overconfidence finding bites hardest exactly when an author reviews their own AI-authored code, which is why the gates are machine-enforced rather than checklist-attested.
- **No independent external verification.** No third-party ASVS review, penetration test, or DAST has run (signal 10). This is not treated as passed. It is an Absent row, held by a dated (2026-06-12) standing risk acceptance in `RELEASE-GATE.md:73-90`. The acceptance explicitly voids on any off-loopback or production exposure. At that point `RELEASE-GATE.md` item #3 and `SDS-REMEDIATION-PLAN.md` require the engagement + DAST to be commissioned before shipping. The `127.0.0.1`-default bind guard (signal 6) is the mechanism that keeps the pre-condition of that acceptance true.
- **Consequence for these grades.** Every score in §2 reflects control presence + evidence honesty at HEAD (2026-07-14), not runtime efficacy under an adversary and not an external challenge. Per the anti-metric rule ([Secure Build Standards §4.1](Secure_Build_Standards.md#41-the-anti-metric-rule-hard)), no release may be gated on any single row, and the composite A- is directional and project-set, not evidence-certified. The dated acceptance is the honest way to carry the capping gap (signal 10): it is recorded, bounded by a trigger, and re-scored, not waved away. The grade is A- because the three items that had held it at B+ are resolved (§1 gives the detail). When an independent engagement lands, this re-grades to A.
